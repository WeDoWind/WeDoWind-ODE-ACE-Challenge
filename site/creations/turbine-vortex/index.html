How the turbine shapes the artwork

Measured RPM sets the swirl. Wind shapes rippling tunnel walls and the pace of travel. Power moves the palette from violet through cyan to warm gold. Rings and streaks rush towards you and pass beyond the screen. Each layer keeps the conditions that formed it, while fading trails leave a phosphor-like echo. These are artistic mappings, not a physical simulation.

Wind and power adapt to their last five minutes of fresh observations. The range adjusts slowly and has a minimum span, letting small changes show without exaggerating tiny fluctuations. Brightness also follows absolute power. The range starts learning when you open this page. Visual history builds over roughly 20–40 seconds of active motion; older layers move past you. The initial tunnel uses the first real reading throughout, then develops its own history.

One combined request every two seconds. Each signal’s observation time is shown in your local timezone. Signals older than 60 seconds are stale; if any signal is stale or unavailable, the artwork rests and any retained numbers are clearly marked. No simulated readings replace live data.
