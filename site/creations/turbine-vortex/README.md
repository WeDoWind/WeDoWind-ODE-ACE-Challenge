# Turbine Vortex

A dependency-free living canvas artwork by charlie9578. Fly through a luminous tunnel shaped by measured rotor speed, wind speed, power and wind-vane angle. Cross-sections remember the conditions that formed them, grow towards the viewer, and pass beyond the screen. Stable main mappings help viewers learn to read the turbine through the artwork. All code, styling and assets belong to this folder; no build, keys or paid services are required.

## Reproduce

From the repository root run `python -m http.server 8000`, then open <http://localhost:8000/site/creations/turbine-vortex/>. To test the Pages path prefix, serve the repository's parent directory and visit `/WeDoWind-ODE-ACE-Challenge/site/creations/turbine-vortex/`. The deployed Pages URL omits `site/`: <https://wedowind.github.io/WeDoWind-ODE-ACE-Challenge/creations/turbine-vortex/>. It becomes available only after upstream merge and Pages deployment.

Pause motion freezes travel, history and trails while leaving live readings updating. Reduced-motion preferences start the artwork paused; Resume motion explicitly opts in. **Immerse** fills the screen; controls fade after six idle seconds and return with movement, touch or keyboard input. Keyboard focus keeps controls visible. Connection status stays visible, and stale/unavailable warnings retain the visible-history data timestamps. Hidden pages stop animation and polling. The optional **Read the wind** guide pairs visual meanings with live markers and labelled scales.

## Live data contract

Docs, OpenAPI, `/v1/sites`, `/v1/sites/ace/assets`, `/v1/families` and a live combined response verified on **13 October 2026**. Site `ace`, asset `wec-1`, family `wec_instantaneous`, resolution `1s`:

<https://ace-api.duckdns.org/v1/sites/ace/assets/wec-1/data/wec_instantaneous/latest?resolution=1s&fields=wec_rotor_speed,wec_wind_speed,wec_active_power,wec_wind_direction&limit=1>

- `wec_rotor_speed`: rpm; `wec_wind_speed`: m/s; `wec_active_power`: kW; `wec_wind_direction`: degrees, native OPC node `Vane`. The catalogue was checked on 13 October 2026; the four-signal live response, per-field observations and collection timestamps were rechecked on 15 October 2026. Signed vane angles are valid; a live response included -11.6 degrees. The catalogue does not specify its geographic reference, so the artwork makes no geographic compass claim.
- One shared, completion-based request every **2 seconds**, above the catalogue's 1-second minimum. Manual refresh shares the cooldown; no overlap. A 15-second timeout, exponential failure backoff up to 60 seconds, and HTTP 429 `Retry-After` apply. Separate visitors/tabs each generate their own traffic.
- Metadata was checked during development and is not repeatedly fetched by visitors. All four fields are requested together with `limit=1`.
- Each field must be finite with case-insensitive `Good` quality and a valid `series_observed_at` Unix microsecond timestamp, converted to milliseconds. Nulls, missing fields, invalid quality, invalid observation dates and regressing observations are rejected. Negative wind/RPM are rejected; signed power and vane angles remain visible. Valid zero is preserved. If the direction signal fails or is stale, the artwork rests rather than inventing a heading.
- **60 seconds** is the chosen stale threshold, not a server guarantee. Freshness uses the gap between each signal and the API data clock (the maximum of observation and valid collection timestamps), plus monotonic `performance.now()` time since that field last advanced. Duplicate responses cannot reset this timer. If collection timestamps are absent, initial absolute age is unknown; the newest observation anchors the relative age and lack of advancement is still detected. No comparison with the computer wall clock is used for validation or freshness; no ahead-of-computer warnings are shown. Old or stalled signals rest the artwork and emit a warning once per stale episode, while polling continues.
- The observation area shows oldest/newest timestamps and their span **for retained tunnel layers**, not time since fetch. Timestamps are from the data, formatted in the viewer's timezone. Raster afterimages can fade for about 1.2 seconds after a layer is discarded and are not counted in that span. Malformed/offline/timeout errors retain labelled readings, show a reason and emit a console warning; no demo fallback is used.
- Automatic polling reschedules when a browser timer fires before its monotonic deadline, rounded up to a whole millisecond. The old early-return path could silently strand the polling loop. Manual refresh shares the same rearming guard, cooldown, backoff and Retry-After restrictions. Tests reproduce early delivery for 10,000 poll cycles, in addition to overlap, stop/resume and rate-limit cases.

## Artistic mapping and adaptive scale

Rotor angular velocity is `RPM × 2π/60 × 1.0`, capped at 30 RPM as an artistic comfort bound. At a steady 12 RPM, the blades complete one revolution in five seconds. Rotation eases towards the measured speed with a 2.5-second time constant. Zero RPM stops rotation; artistic forward travel can continue when all signals are fresh. The travelling wake retains its additional artistic depth twist.

The main meanings stay stable across visits: wind travel uses `clamp(wind/15)` and power uses `clamp(power/4200)`. Fifteen m/s is an artistic scale ceiling, not a rated turbine limit. The 4200 kW value is the nominal turbine power documented in the API introduction. Negative power remains visible but maps to the zero-generation colour. Wind and power each also use five minutes of fresh, distinct observations for small detail accents. Their 10th–90th percentile ranges resist spikes; the range adapts with a 30-second time constant and minimum spans of **1.5 m/s** and **250 kW**. Adaptive power changes ring hue by at most ±6 degrees. Absolute wind modulates light-wave spacing. Adaptation does not remap the main flight speed, palette or streak density. Duplicate observations do not overweight the history. No historical API request is made. Rotation, wind, colour and detail targets ease over 2.5 seconds; brightness uses a faster 0.6-second time constant.

Wind controls light-wave spacing and camera speed `0.4 + 1.2 × clamp(wind/15)` in artistic depth units per second. Fixed power colour uses `sqrt(clamp(power/4200))` to move from hue 280 (violet) to 190 (cyan) to 40 (gold), revealing detail at low output without adapting the main meaning. The guide labels 0, 250, 1000, 2500 and 4200 kW on that same scale. Higher absolute power also creates more bright streaks. Brightness instead shows deviation from a **time-weighted trailing 30-second power mean**, calculated from distinct observation timestamps as a piecewise-constant signal. The current sample has zero duration when it first arrives; earlier readings contribute according to how long they applied. Brightness is `0.5 + 0.35 × clamp((power - mean) / max(25 kW, abs(mean) × 0.05), -1, 1)`: above-average power brightens, below-average power dims, and the mean is neutral. The minimum 25 kW span limits noise amplification. The readout shows the mean and signed difference, with a warming-up label until 30 seconds of observations are available. A gap longer than 60 seconds resets the mean. Each emitted layer retains its original relative brightness as it travels. These mappings express conditions rather than simulate physics or energy totals.

Vane direction is wrapped to [-180,180) for rendering, with an eight-second time constant and shortest-arc easing across wraparound. Each layer remembers its angle. Sine/cosine project it onto a bounded screen bend, strongest in the distance; historical bends travel towards the viewer. Screen-up/left/right are artistic orientations, not geographic bearings. No nacelle angle or unverified compass transformation is inferred.

`tunnel.mjs` keeps an in-memory sequence of cross-sections. Every 0.65 seconds of active animation a new layer stores the smoothed wind, power, vane angle, brightness, detail accents, rotor-driven twist and current observation time. Those attributes remain attached to it as it approaches the viewer. Perspective expansion makes layers and bright beads pass out of the frame. Thirty-two fine wall filaments provide context; **three brighter wake strands begin at the exact tips of a three-blade rotor**. The rotor and calm cross-sections are circular in equal pixel coordinates, without vertical squash. This is an artistic tip-wake depiction, not a fluid-dynamics simulation. Wind-dependent travel yields roughly **17–69 seconds of active visual history**; layers are discarded when their complete wake envelope passes beyond the furthest viewport corner, or crosses the near plane. This can shorten the visible span from the depth-only limit, depending on screen shape and wind. Storage is also capped at 140 layers; expired layers have no persistent references. The five-minute adaptive buffers are capped at 512 samples each, and the 30-second power buffer at 256 samples (normally far fewer at two-second polling). Startup geometry is initialized together from the first live state, not a fabricated historical feed. History then develops from readings received during this visit, without extra API requests or persistent storage.

The canvas uses frame-rate-independent exponential fading with a 0.26-second time constant, leaving visible phosphor-like echoes for about 1.2 seconds. Rendering is capped at 30fps, with geometry reused across rings and wall filaments and device-pixel ratio capped at two. This limits raster work during long sessions; actual frame rate depends on hardware. Pause stops both layer movement and trail decay. Stale/unavailable data draws a still subdued tunnel; recovery resumes the retained layers. Resizing clears pixel trails but retains layer history. No external images or rendering libraries are used.

## Local smooth-wake experiment

This local branch retains the 1.0 RPM multiplier. Spiral segments use continuous-tangent cubic curves instead of straight chords. The three tip trails are sampled between historical layers (3–16 subdivisions, depending on angular/depth separation) before curve drawing. Subdivisions are transient geometry, not extra retained history.

Projection uses a single world unit, `1.25 × min(canvas width, canvas height)`, for radius and both bend axes, anchored at the exact viewport centre. Swapping portrait and landscape no longer stretches the bend direction or magnitude relative to the rotor. A larger window naturally reveals/rescales the scene.

The blade-tip trails are drawn without any dark rotor mask, so their origins remain visible. The wake alone uses a reusable canvas for afterimage persistence; the rotor is composited fresh on top, so blades cannot smear into a halo. This adds one fixed canvas buffer, resized with the viewport, without accumulating frames. No decorative eddies are drawn. Wind change between distinct observations (m/s per second) controls polygonal roughness: `sqrt(clamp(abs(change)/0.5))`, eased over 1.2 seconds. Constant wind targets zero roughness. A retained layer blends from a circle into an irregular 12-sided polygon, and its connecting curves become angular with fewer interpolated subdivisions. Deformation grows over the first three depth units downstream, leaving the blade-tip connections exact. Historical rough layers travel out of view as smooth readings replace them; there is no random frame-by-frame jitter. Rough-layer disposal conservatively allows for the polygon's smaller inner radius, with an 8-pixel stroke margin and the existing storage caps.

When readings are unavailable, stale, or not yet received, the canvas shows a static, centred straight tunnel with concentric circles and radial spokes. It has no rotor, bend or twist and does not invent observations. Retained live history is frozen and can resume when data returns. Pausing valid live data still freezes the actual artwork.

The rotor is anchored at the exact canvas centre at every vane angle. The wake's bend uses a depth envelope `4d(1-d)` with `d=z/28`, so displacement is zero at the rotor and viewer. Horizontal displacement uses `sin(angle)` and vertical displacement uses `1-cos(angle)`; a neutral zero-degree reading produces no bend on either axis. These are artistic screen coordinates, not compass directions.
